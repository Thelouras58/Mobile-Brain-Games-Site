**To play a 1–25 Schulte-style number grid, find and select every number in ascending order, beginning with 1 and ending with 25.** The numbers are shuffled, so the task is to locate the next value rather than read across each row.

## What are the basic rules?

1. Look over the shuffled 5×5 grid.
2. Find **1** and select it.
3. Find **2**, then **3**, and continue in numerical order.
4. Finish when you select **25**.

A digital version may time the round or show the next number to find. Those interface details can vary, but the central rule stays the same: select the complete sequence without skipping ahead.

## A worked 1-to-5 example

In the illustrative grid above, **1** is in the first row, fourth column. After selecting it, move to **2** in the third row, first column. **3** appears in the bottom-right corner. The next target, **4**, is in the third row, fifth column, and **5** is in the second row, third column.

Notice that the sequence jumps between distant parts of the grid. Reading each row from left to right repeatedly can work, but it may require covering the same cells many times. Another approach is to keep the entire grid in view and scan outward from a comfortable central point. Use whichever method makes the rule easiest to follow; this is a game, not a standardized assessment.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Starting with the most visible number.** The first target is always 1, even when another value stands out.
- **Skipping a number.** Selecting 7 after 5 does not complete the required sequence; locate 6 first.
- **Guessing under pressure.** If the game records time, accuracy still depends on checking the target before selecting it.
- **Treating one time as a diagnosis.** A result describes that round under those conditions. It does not measure a person's overall attention, reading ability, or health.
