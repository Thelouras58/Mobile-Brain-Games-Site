**A streak shows that you returned to a game. It does not show that your memory has improved outside the game.** A short, enjoyable card-matching round can be a simple way to build a play routine, and an in-game calendar can make that routine easier to notice.

## What does a daily streak measure?

A streak counts days on which you played. It is useful as a record of participation, not a test of cognitive ability. A missed day says nothing about a player's memory. If a child enjoys matching cards, the goal can simply be to play together when there is time.

## Which game stats are useful?

In-game times, scores, and completed rounds show what happened **in that game**. They may help you choose a comfortable difficulty or notice which card theme a player enjoys. Scores from different boards or difficulty levels may not be directly comparable.

## How can I make a simple family routine?

1. Choose a difficulty that is easy to understand.
2. Play one round and talk about a pair you both noticed.
3. Stop when the game is no longer enjoyable.
4. Return another day if the player asks to play again.

The routine is a way to enjoy a game together. It does not need a daily target or a promise of wider learning benefits.

## Does a memory-game streak improve memory?

A streak alone provides no evidence that memory improves in everyday life. It can encourage someone to return to a card-matching activity, and practice may make that particular game feel more familiar. Avoid treating the calendar or a score as a health assessment.
